Click the game to start. Arrows move the cursor, Space places a toy, hold Space and press left/right to choose a toy, Enter is Game Reset, G is Game Select. P pauses, M mutes.

How to playThree shelves, nine slots, one toy box to protect.

  1. Pick a slot. The joystick moves the cursor over the nine slots, three on each shelf. An empty slot shows a blinking ghost of the toy you're about to place.
  2. Choose a toy. Hold fire and push left or right to flip through the toys you've unlocked.
  3. Place it. Press fire on an empty slot. It costs batteries, and it can't go down on a monster that is already standing there. Fire on a toy picks it up again and gives you half its cost back.
  4. Keep the batteries coming. One trickles in every four seconds, and every monster you stop pays one or two more (a T-Rex pays five). They're tight: spend them carefully. Watch the big green number on the status line.
  5. Guard the box. The first monster to reach the toy box slams that shelf's lid and clears the shelf. The lid only slams once: the next monster through the same shelf ends the game.

Your toysA new toy unlocks as the waves go on. Only one shot flies along a shelf at a time, so toys on the same shelf take turns.

Army man

10 batteriesfrom wave 1
health 8

Fires along its shelf about every 0.7 seconds, 1 damage a bullet. Cheap and steady, but its bullets fly over a crawler that's busy chewing.

Teddy

5 batteriesfrom wave 2
health 40

Doesn't fight back. Monsters stop to chew on it, which holds a dino up for about ten seconds while your other toys work.

Tank

25 batteriesfrom wave 4
health 12

One heavy shell every 5 seconds: 8 damage, enough for most monsters. Shells can't reach the balloon clown.

Cowboy

15 batteriesfrom wave 5
health 8

Lassos a monster up to 40 pixels ahead, far enough to catch one chewing the toy in front, and holds it still for a second, every two seconds. Great for stopping a charging knight.

Cannon

20 batteriesfrom wave 7
health 10

A cannonball every 3.3 seconds, 3 damage, and the blast splashes the same damage onto any monster crowded right behind or beside it. It hits everything, balloon clowns included.

Jet

30 batteriesfrom wave 9
one use

Takes off from the toy box end as soon as you place it and flies the whole shelf in about a second, hitting every monster it passes for 10 damage. Then it's gone: save it for emergencies.

Game Select

Before a game the status line reads GAME 1. Press Game Select to step through the three games, then fire or Game Reset to start. Game 1 starts at wave 1 with 30 batteries, game 2 at wave 5 with 50, game 3 at wave 9 with 70 and every toy. Pressing Select during a game ends it and takes you back to choosing.

Difficulty switch

Leave the left difficulty switch on B for the normal climb. On A the monsters move at their lap-two speed and arrive more often, right from wave 1. You can flip it any time.

Score

Every monster you stop is worth points, from 5 for a mouse to 50 for a T-Rex. The status line shows your score and the wave in gold, and your batteries as the big green number.

Tips from the toy box

  • Put an army man on every shelf in wave 1. The first dinos come one or two at a time.
  • A teddy in front of your shooters buys them time: the monsters stop to chew, your army men keep firing.
  • Toys on the same shelf share one shot, so spread your shooters across the shelves rather than stacking one shelf.
  • Don't count on the lid. It saves a shelf once; after that, a single monster through ends the game.
  • Get a tank or cannon onto each shelf soon after wave 4, when crawlers start sneaking under your army men's fire.
  • Monsters pile up behind a teddy while they chew. A cannon on that shelf splashes the whole pile.
  • Picking a toy up refunds half its cost. Move a teddy to whichever shelf is in trouble.
  • Save 30 batteries for a jet once you reach wave 9. It's your answer to a crowded shelf.

About the cartridge

Toy Wars is a real Atari 2600 program, written in 6502 assembly for a 16K cartridge (F6 bank switching) with Atari's Super Chip, 128 extra bytes of RAM. It runs here on a 6502 and TIA emulator, cycle by cycle, at 60 frames a second. The cartridge image also runs in Stella.

Every frame it draws is checked against a reference picture made from the game's state, and every rule is checked by automated tests.
